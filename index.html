Case study 01 · Platform transformation

Building a Scalable Knowledge Platform for Customer Self-Service

I led the evaluation and transition from MindTouch to Document360 to address search and navigation pain points, improve the organization of technical information, and establish a stronger foundation for AI-powered search and customer self-service.

Customer experience · Knowledge management · AI

Making technical information easier to find

My role: documentation initiative owner and migration lead, aligning platform decisions with customer needs and business priorities.

The challenge

Customer feedback and internal stakeholder comments highlighted friction in the technical documentation portal, particularly around finding relevant information and navigating the content. The goal was to make search more effective and organize knowledge so users could reach the right sections with less effort.

What I did

  1. Defined the evaluation criteria. Turned customer feedback and internal requirements into practical use cases for assessing search, information organization, customization, integrations, and AI capabilities.
  2. Compared platforms against real workflows. Evaluated multiple authoring options against the use cases and operational needs, rather than choosing on features alone.
  3. Led the platform transition. Selected Document360 and coordinated the move of Help content to establish a more capable foundation for content discovery, navigation, and maintenance.

Value created

  • Improved search experience: The new platform delivered a noticeable improvement in search, making it easier for users to find relevant technical information.
  • Clearer information architecture: Better-organized content helped users navigate the documentation and access the appropriate sections more easily.
  • Foundation for AI-powered self-service: Established a stronger platform base for future AI-enabled knowledge discovery, with customer and internal stakeholder feedback informing ongoing improvements.

How this supports customer and business outcomes

Customer success

A more capable documentation platform gives customers a clearer route to relevant product guidance when they need to complete a task or resolve a question.

Self-service

Improved search and clearer content organization make it easier for users to discover relevant guidance and navigate directly to the sections they need.

Operational efficiency

Search and usage analytics create a practical basis for prioritizing content fixes instead of relying only on ad hoc requests.

AI readiness

The platform transition establishes a stronger foundation for structured knowledge discovery and future AI-enabled experiences; it does not by itself prove AI performance gains.

Qualitative improvements in search and navigation were observed, alongside internal stakeholder feedback. Quantified effects on search success, support volume, or customer outcomes have not yet been established.

Evidence status: The reported improvements are qualitative: better search, stronger information organization, and easier navigation to relevant sections. Next-step measures could include search-success rates, unsuccessful searches, user feedback, and documentation-related support trends.

Case study 02 · AI-assisted workflows

Operationalizing AI in Documentation Workflows

I transformed a predominantly manual documentation process into a structured, AI-assisted workflow. As the agent matured, it reduced first-draft effort, streamlined editorial review, and helped writers analyse Jira stories and product changes more efficiently. This enabled faster responses to documentation requests and more time for high-value editorial and strategic work, with writers retaining responsibility for technical accuracy and final quality.

1. Analyse product changes

Use structured prompts to examine product information, Jira stories, linked issues, and relevant context to identify documentation implications and user needs.

2. Develop a first draft

Guide AI-assisted drafting using product terminology, content standards, and the intended user task to create a starting point for writers.

3. Review and refine

Apply a separate review stage to assess completeness, structure, clarity, and alignment with product behaviour and documentation standards.

4. Retain human ownership

Writers validate technical details and resolve gaps with subject-matter experts. I review, edit, or write content directly when needed to maintain quality.

Approach: AI supports the workflow; it does not replace technical validation, editorial judgment, or writer accountability. Time savings have not been formally quantified.

How this improves the content operation

Faster drafting

A trained AI-assisted workflow gives writers a stronger starting draft, reducing manual first-draft effort and helping the team respond to documentation requests faster.

Consistency and review efficiency

Structured drafting and a dedicated review stage support more consistent structure, terminology, and documentation standards while reducing effort spent strengthening initial drafts.

More efficient Jira analysis

AI agents help writers analyse Jira stories, linked issues, and product changes more efficiently, improving documentation planning and helping identify user-facing content needs.

Higher-value editorial work

By shifting effort away from manual content generation, writers can spend more time validating technical details, improving quality, and designing content around customer needs—while retaining human ownership of accuracy.

These are qualitative operational outcomes; time savings and quality improvements have not yet been quantified. Future measurement could track drafting time, review effort, turnaround time, and revision rounds.

Case study 03 · Go Green initiative

Using Customer Insights to Drive Documentation Improvement

I led content modernization through the Go Green initiative by generating a Top Viewed Topics report, assessing how old the content was, and sharing the findings with Product Managers and Engineering. Working with stakeholders, we identified priority topics, analyzed content gaps, refreshed the material, improved its visual presentation, and added use cases to every top-viewed topic.

Customer insights · Content quality · Analytics

Making content maintenance more intentional

My role: lead the Go Green initiative, coordinate with Product Managers and Engineering, and guide evidence-based prioritization and content improvements.

The challenge

Older documentation needed a more systematic refresh, while the move to a new authoring platform created an opportunity to modernize the overall customer experience. Because the Document360 migration was completed only last month, the new platform is still too recent for meaningful performance conclusions.

What I did

  1. Built a prioritization report. Generated a Top Viewed Topics report and assessed content age to identify frequently accessed material that could be outdated.
  2. Aligned cross-functional stakeholders. Shared the report with Product Managers and Engineering, then collaborated to agree on priority topics and understand content gaps.
  3. Refreshed and enriched content. Updated the identified topics, improved their visual presentation, and added use cases to all top-viewed topics to make guidance more practical.
  4. Connected content work to the platform transition. The initiative complemented the move from MindTouch to Document360, supporting a more organized and maintainable documentation experience.

Outcomes and next steps

  • Modernized the documentation experience: Completed the move to Document360 and established a new platform for ongoing content delivery and maintenance.
  • Targeted maintenance: Used topic popularity and content age to guide refresh priorities.
  • More actionable guidance: Added use cases across all top-viewed topics and enhanced their visual presentation.
  • Stronger cross-functional alignment: Shared the report with Product Managers and Engineering and collaborated on topic selection and content-gap analysis.
  • Measurement discipline: Continue to evaluate usage and customer feedback as sufficient post-migration data becomes available.

How this supports customer success and efficiency

Customer success

Prioritizing high-traffic topics and assessing their age helps keep frequently used guidance current and relevant.

Self-service

Adding use cases and improving visual presentation makes frequently accessed guidance more practical to navigate and apply.

Operational efficiency

Using page-view evidence to prioritize updates gives the team a repeatable way to focus maintenance effort where it is most likely to matter.

Continuous improvement

The new platform and early customer conversations provide a starting point for monitoring content usage and identifying future improvement areas.

Customer conversations were positive, but measurable changes in support volume, satisfaction, or search success have not yet been established.

Measurement status: The migration is recent and platform metrics have not yet been evaluated. No quantified improvement in search, adoption, satisfaction, or support volume is claimed at this stage.

Case study 05 · Documentation leadership

Leading Documentation Strategy, Operations, and Team Development

I lead documentation strategy, operations, team development, and quality practices while aligning knowledge investments with product priorities and customer success goals. I identified opportunities for AI-assisted work, established a structured adoption approach, trained the team, and integrated the workflow into everyday documentation practices.

Function ownership · Team development · Cross-functional delivery

Connecting strategy, people, and execution

My role: own the Product Documentation function’s quality, maturity, and business impact while leading a team of four Product Documentation Specialists.

The leadership challenge

Documentation work spans product changes, release commitments, customer questions, and ongoing content maintenance. The team needs clear priorities and consistent quality while requirements and stakeholder needs evolve.

How I lead

  1. Translate priorities into delivery. Connect business and product goals to documentation priorities, release notes, FAQs, and customer tasks.
  2. Develop the team and enable AI adoption. Identified opportunities to use AI in documentation, defined a structured approach to adoption, trained writers on effective usage, and established quality expectations. Coach writers to shift from feature-led descriptions to goal-based, customer-first content.
  3. Stay close to the craft. Review, edit, and write content when needed to resolve gaps, strengthen information architecture, and maintain editorial quality.
  4. Align cross-functional partners. Work with R&D, Technical Support, Services, and product stakeholders to clarify product behaviour and keep content aligned with customer needs.

Leadership outcomes

  • Introduced AI-assisted workflows that reduced drafting effort and gave writers more time for validation and content quality.
  • Shifted documentation toward customer goals and outcomes rather than feature descriptions alone.
  • Strengthened collaboration across Product, Support, and Engineering to align product changes with documentation priorities.
  • Established consistent documentation and review practices to support quality, knowledge sharing, and team effectiveness.
  • Balanced strategic direction, operational delivery, team development, and hands-on editorial leadership.

This player-coach approach keeps strategy connected to execution while maintaining human ownership of technical accuracy, editorial judgment, and final content quality.

How this creates value beyond content delivery

Customer success

Aligning documentation with customer tasks helps shift content from describing features to helping users achieve outcomes.

Operational efficiency

Clear priorities and coaching help the team coordinate work across releases, FAQs, maintenance, and stakeholder requests.

Consistent quality

Hands-on reviews, shared expectations, and stronger information architecture support a more coherent customer experience across content types.

Scalable capability

Developing four specialists builds team capability and distributes documentation expertise instead of concentrating decisions with one person.
