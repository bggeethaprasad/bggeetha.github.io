Case Study 02

Operationalizing AI in Documentation Workflows

The goal is not to replace technical writers with AI. It is to reduce repetitive work, structure the workflow, and give writers more time for technical understanding, editorial judgment, and customer-focused content.

AI-enabled content operations

A multi-agent workflow with human review at the centre

Using AI to support product analysis, first drafts, review, and Jira-related task analysis—while keeping authors responsible for final quality.

The challenge

Documentation work often involves gathering information from product changes, analysing requirements, drafting content, checking style and purpose, and reviewing linked work items. These steps can take time when handled manually and inconsistently.

The workflow

1. Product analysis

An analysis agent reviews available product information and requirements to identify the purpose, audience, key changes, and documentation needs.

2. AI-assisted drafting

A drafting workflow uses the analysis to prepare an initial version aligned with the intended audience, task, and content format.

3. Editorial review

A review agent checks the draft against its goal, style, clarity, consistency, and completeness, then identifies improvements.

4. Jira task analysis

A Jira agent analyses linked issues and subtasks, gathers relevant context, and helps prepare documentation for the author's final review.

Human accountability and governance

  • AI-generated content remains a draft until reviewed by the author.
  • Technical accuracy must be checked against authoritative product information.
  • Writers retain control over the final wording, structure, and publication decision.
  • Workflow outputs are assessed for clarity, audience relevance, style, and purpose—not simply speed.

Business value

The workflow reduces repetitive analysis and drafting effort, brings more structure to review, and allows writers to focus on accuracy, editorial judgment, and customer outcomes.

Key takeaway: AI adds value when it is embedded in a defined process with clear review responsibilities and human accountability.

Case Study 03

Using Customer Insights to Improve Documentation

Prioritizing documentation around customer needs helps teams invest effort where it can make the greatest difference.

Content strategy

Turning customer signals into content priorities

Using available feedback and content signals to guide continuous improvement.

The challenge

As products evolve, documentation can become outdated, gaps can emerge, and content may not always reflect the questions customers are trying to answer.

My approach

  • Use available customer feedback and Support insights to understand recurring questions and friction points.
  • Review content usage and search signals where available.
  • Prioritize content updates based on customer value, product changes, and content relevance.
  • Work with product stakeholders to validate changes and maintain accuracy.

Business value

This approach creates a more deliberate content improvement cycle, helping the team focus on relevant customer tasks and keep documentation aligned with product changes.

Case Study 04

Preparing Knowledge for AI-Powered Self-Service

AI-powered knowledge experiences are only as useful as the content they retrieve. Structured, accurate, task-oriented knowledge provides a stronger foundation for relevant answers.

Knowledge readiness

Building the foundation for more discoverable knowledge

Connecting documentation structure, product accuracy, and customer self-service.

The challenge

AI-powered search and conversational self-service need knowledge that is easy to retrieve, clearly scoped, consistent, and grounded in trustworthy product information.

My approach

  • Partner with Support and Product stakeholders to identify knowledge gaps and recurring customer questions.
  • Promote clear titles, task-focused structure, consistent terminology, and concise answers.
  • Keep content aligned with product behaviour and authoritative information.
  • Consider discoverability and maintainability when developing or revising knowledge assets.

Business value

These practices support a more structured, discoverable knowledge base that can serve both conventional self-service and evolving AI-powered experiences.

Key takeaway: AI readiness begins with trustworthy knowledge, clear structure, and ongoing content governance.

My Approach

A Practical, Customer-First Framework

I connect customer needs, product knowledge, and content operations through a repeatable approach that keeps documentation useful, accurate, discoverable, and aligned with business priorities.

1. Understand the user

Start with customer goals, common questions, support cases, and the tasks users need to complete.

2. Connect content to product

Work with Product, Engineering, and Support to understand behaviour, identify gaps, and maintain accuracy.

3. Design for discovery

Use clear information architecture, meaningful titles, consistent terminology, and practical examples.

4. Prioritize with evidence

Use available page views, search patterns, customer feedback, and content age to guide improvements.

5. Apply AI responsibly

Use AI for analysis and drafting, with technical validation and human ownership of the final content.

6. Improve continuously

Review feedback and content performance, address gaps, and refine knowledge as products evolve.
