Case study 01 · Platform transformation

Modernizing the Documentation Experience

I led the evaluation and transition to a new documentation platform to improve how customers discover technical information and establish a foundation for AI-enabled search and ongoing content improvement.

Customer experience · Knowledge management · AI

Making technical information easier to find

My role: documentation initiative owner and migration lead, aligning platform decisions with customer needs and business priorities.

The challenge

Customer surveys and stakeholder feedback highlighted opportunities to improve the search experience on the technical documentation portal. Customers needed a more effective way to discover relevant, accurate information.

What I did

  1. Defined the evaluation criteria. Turned customer feedback and internal requirements into practical use cases for assessing search, information organization, customization, integrations, and AI capabilities.
  2. Compared platforms against real workflows. Evaluated multiple authoring options against the use cases and operational needs, rather than choosing on features alone.
  3. Led the platform transition. Selected Document360 and coordinated the move of Help content to establish a more capable foundation for content discovery and maintenance.

Value created

  • Stronger platform foundation: Consolidated Help content in a platform with enhanced search and analytics capabilities.
  • Clearer path to continuous improvement: Created the opportunity to use search behaviour and customer feedback to prioritize future content updates.
Measurement status: Customer impact has not yet been formally measured. Useful follow-up measures include unsuccessful searches, search relevance, feedback, and documentation-related support trends.

Case study 02 · AI-assisted workflows

Building a Structured AI-Assisted Documentation Workflow

I designed a repeatable Gemini workflow that supports product analysis, first-draft development, and content review. My role combines workflow design and guidance for writers with human editorial oversight and accountability for quality.

1. Analyse product changes

Use structured prompts to examine product information, Jira stories, linked issues, and relevant context to identify documentation implications and user needs.

2. Develop a first draft

Guide AI-assisted drafting using product terminology, content standards, and the intended user task to create a starting point for writers.

3. Review and refine

Apply a separate review stage to assess completeness, structure, clarity, and alignment with product behaviour and documentation standards.

4. Retain human ownership

Writers validate technical details and resolve gaps with subject-matter experts. I review, edit, or write content directly when needed to maintain quality.

Approach: AI supports the workflow; it does not replace technical validation, editorial judgment, or writer accountability. Time savings have not been formally quantified.

Case study 03 · Go Green initiative

Refreshing High-Interest and Outdated Documentation

I led content-currency improvements through the Go Green initiative, using top-viewed-topic analysis to prioritize updates. The work complemented the recent migration from MindTouch to Document360 and created a foundation for evaluating the new documentation experience over time.

Customer insights · Content quality · Analytics

Making content maintenance more intentional

My role: lead the Go Green documentation improvement initiative and guide the team in prioritizing content updates.

The challenge

Older documentation needed a more systematic refresh, while the move to a new authoring platform created an opportunity to modernize the overall customer experience. Because the Document360 migration was completed only last month, the new platform is still too recent for meaningful performance conclusions.

What I did

  1. Prioritized content using evidence. Used top-viewed-topic reporting and documentation reviews to identify the topics customers accessed most and focus refresh work where it mattered.
  2. Delivered content improvements. Updated all identified top-viewed topics through the Go Green initiative, improving content currency for frequently visited subjects.
  3. Modernized the platform experience. Completed the migration from MindTouch to Document360, establishing a new platform for maintaining and evolving customer-facing documentation.
  4. Listened to early customer feedback. Spoke with 2–3 customers after the migration and received positive feedback on the new portal and the updated documentation approach.

Outcomes and next steps

  • Modernized the documentation experience: Completed the move to Document360 and established a new platform for ongoing content delivery and maintenance.
  • Improved content currency: Updated all identified top-viewed topics as part of the Go Green initiative.
  • Gathered early customer validation: Received positive feedback from 2–3 customer conversations about the new portal and approach.
  • Established a measurement baseline: Plan to review platform usage, customer feedback, and content performance as sufficient data becomes available.
Measurement status: The migration is recent and platform metrics have not yet been evaluated. No quantified improvement in search, adoption, satisfaction, or support volume is claimed at this stage.

Case study 04 · Conversational AI knowledge

Preparing Product Knowledge for Conversational AI

Effective conversational AI depends on relevant, clear, and useful knowledge. My contribution focuses on helping Support translate customer questions and practical troubleshooting experience into knowledge that can support a customer-facing AI assistant.

Represent real customer questions

Worked with Support to develop questions and answers grounded in customer needs, common scenarios, and the information users need to understand and use the product.

Capture practical troubleshooting guidance

Prepare knowledge for customer self-service

Case study 05 · Documentation leadership

Leading Product Documentation from Strategy to Delivery

I balance accountability for documentation outcomes with team development and hands-on editorial leadership, translating product and business priorities into work the team can deliver.

Function ownership · Team development · Cross-functional delivery

Connecting strategy, people, and execution

My role: own the Product Documentation function’s quality, maturity, and business impact while leading a team of four Product Documentation Specialists.

The leadership challenge

Documentation work spans product changes, release commitments, customer questions, and ongoing content maintenance. The team needs clear priorities and consistent quality while requirements and stakeholder needs evolve.

How I lead

  1. Translate priorities into delivery. Connect business and product goals to documentation priorities, release notes, FAQs, and customer tasks.
  2. Develop the team. Coach writers based on their strengths and development needs, set clear expectations, and encourage a shift from feature-led descriptions to goal-based, customer-first content.
  3. Stay close to the craft. Review, edit, and write content when needed to resolve gaps, strengthen information architecture, and maintain editorial quality.
  4. Align cross-functional partners. Work with R&D, Technical Support, Services, and product stakeholders to clarify product behaviour and keep content aligned with customer needs.

Value of the approach

This player-coach model keeps strategy connected to execution: the team has direction and support, while documentation decisions remain grounded in product context, customer needs, and editorial judgment.
